GOALACT — UI direction references

Real apps worth showing the client, grouped by design direction. Click any screenshot to open it full-size. The question for the client: which of these should GoalAct feel like?

Recommended

A · Score-first broadcast

Dark, dense, card-based. Built for the matchday loop: open app → see live score → check round → table. Our v2 already follows this pattern.

FotMob - Soccer Live Scores

The benchmark. Look at: pinned score header, tabbed match centre (timeline/lineups/table), date-strip fixtures, form pills in standings.

Sofascore: Live Sports Scores

Same genre, heavier stats. Look at: how much density is too much — useful boundary marker for the client conversation.

App Store ↗

Flashscore: Live Scores & News

The pure list-scanner. Look at: result rows and grouping; minimal chrome, maximum speed.

App Store ↗
Media layer

B · Editorial / video-first

Magazine feed, big thumbnails, polls. Don't redesign the whole app this way — steal it for the Videos, News and Goal-of-the-Week surfaces.

OneFootball Live Soccer scores

Look at: feed cards with big imagery, poll/vote cards (maps to our GOTW), short-form video rows, player profile tabs.

App Store ↗
Contrast option

C · Minimal typographic

Apple's take: plain rows, huge numerals, near-zero chrome. Useful as the "less is more" anchor — cheapest to build, least league identity.

Apple Sports

Look at: typographic score rows, restrained colour, how little UI is needed to scan a whole round.

App Store ↗
Alternate feel

D · League-official

What an official league app looks like: photography, club identity, editorial home. The alternative if the client wants "our league's own product" over a score app.

Premier League - Scores, News

Look at: hero imagery, club colour usage, content-first home. Heavier to produce — needs real photography.

The one question for the client

  1. A — FotMob-style (scores first, dark): fastest path, matches v2, expected pattern.
  2. D — League-official: more identity, more content production cost.
  3. C — Apple Sports minimal: cheapest, least distinctive.

Whichever they pick, B (OneFootball-style cards) applies to Videos / News / Goal of the Week regardless.
